In the summer, it is typical to see a sounding profile in the late morning that has the following layers from the surface upwards: 

    1. An unstable layer near the surface, as the surface warms. Here, we would expect $-\frac{dT_{atm}}{dz} > \Gamma,$ where $\Gamma = 9.8 \text{ K/km.}$ In this scenario, we can easily say that the top of the boundary layer is at the top of this unstable layer.


    2. An inversion above the unstable layer, which is very stable. In this region, we would expect $-\frac{dT_{atm}}{dz} > 0$



However, this situation will not always be the case!

There are two directories, one labeled Hawthorne_soundings and the other labeled Airport_soundings. They contain CSV files.

* Hawthorne_soundings contains radiosonde observations taken during the USOS Campaign at approximately 17:00:00 PM UTC, or 11 AM Mountain Time. They do not have data for every day.

* Airport_soundings contains radiosonde observations taken near the Salt Lake City International Airport by the National Weather Service at 00z (00:00:00 AM UTC) and 12Z (12:00:00 PM UTC) every day, which corresponds to 6 PM and 6 AM Mountain Time. This means the file labeled 20240731_00z should correspond to July 30th, 2024 at 6 PM Mountain Time and the file labeled 20240731_12z should correspond to July 31st, 2024 at 6 AM Mountain Time.
